beadMaterials:
  - title: Wood
    text: >-
      Wood is lightweight, warm to the touch, and well suited to traditional, missionary, men's, and natural-style collections. Grain, knots, density, and shade can vary between beads and production batches. Buyers should confirm whether a product uses solid wood, laminated wood, compressed wood, or another material with a wood-grain finish. Also specify staining, paint, wax, lacquer, engraving, and acceptable color variation.
  - title: Glass and crystal-style glass
    text: >-
      Glass beads generally feel heavier and more reflective than wood or plastic. They can be transparent, opaque, faceted, pearl-coated, painted, or internally colored. "Crystal" is sometimes used as a style or trade description, so buyers who require a particular glass composition or cut should request written confirmation. Check hole quality, edge chipping, coating adhesion, color consistency, and breakage protection in packaging.
  - title: Acrylic, plastic, resin, PVC, and silicone
    text: >-
      These names describe different material families and should not be treated as interchangeable. Acrylic and other molded plastics are useful for lightweight, colorful, price-sensitive programs. Resin can support embedded images, handcrafted effects, and complex shapes. Soft PVC and silicone are used for flexible charms and children-oriented designs. Confirm the exact polymer, hardness, odor, colorfastness, molding marks, and destination-market requirements.
  - title: Natural stone and stone-look beads
    text: >-
      Natural stone offers distinctive color, pattern, cool touch, and premium weight, but natural variation is expected. Some beads may be dyed, heat-treated, stabilized, reconstructed, composite, or made from glass or resin to imitate stone. If authenticity is central to your product claim, define the accepted material and treatment in writing and consider independent testing.
  - title: Ceramic, porcelain, shell, and pearl-style materials
    text: >-
      Ceramic and porcelain beads allow painted devotional patterns and a smooth, substantial feel, but require careful packaging against impact. Pearl-style products may use coated glass, coated plastic, shell-based material, or genuine cultured pearl. The appearance can be similar in photos while cost, weight, and care requirements differ greatly.
metalMaterials:
  - title: Zinc alloy
    text: >-
      Zinc alloy is common for cast crucifixes, medals, centerpieces, and decorative charms because it can reproduce complex shapes and accept many surface finishes. The visible antique silver, gold, bronze, or black appearance is usually a finish — not proof of the base metal. Confirm alloy requirements, plating system, topcoat, wear expectations, and limits for lead, cadmium, nickel release, or other restricted substances required by the destination market.
  - title: Stainless steel
    text: >-
      Stainless steel is used for chain, pins, jump rings, crosses, medals, and laser-cut components where durability and corrosion resistance are priorities. Stainless steel is a family of grades, not one material. Grade 304 is widely used; grade 316 contains molybdenum and generally offers stronger resistance in chloride-rich environments. Lower-cost grades may behave differently. Ask for the grade and confirm whether every component is stainless steel or only the main visible part.
  - title: Brass
    text: >-
      Brass is suitable for stamped, formed, machined, and plated components and can deliver a more premium positioning. It may be polished, antiqued, lacquered, silver plated, or gold toned. "Brass color" does not necessarily mean solid brass. Confirm the base metal, copper alloy requirement, plating, protective coating, and any lead restriction that applies to your market.
  - title: Iron, carbon steel, aluminum, and precious metals
    text: >-
      Iron or carbon-steel chain, eye pins, and jump rings can provide good strength at a competitive cost, but exposed areas may corrode if plating or coating is damaged. Aluminum is lightweight and can be anodized, but it has a different feel and hardness from steel. Sterling silver and other precious-metal claims require clear fineness, hallmark, weight, and testing requirements. Do not infer the base metal from color alone.

checklist:
  - 'List the exact material required for every major component — not only the beads.'
  - 'Confirm metal grade, base metal, plating, coating, and surface finish in writing.'
  - 'State the destination country, intended user, and required regulatory or retailer standard.'
  - 'Request clear photos, specifications, and a physical sample before a sensitive bulk order.'
  - 'Approve acceptable color, grain, handmade, and dimensional tolerances.'
  - 'Use qualified third-party testing when composition or compliance is commercially critical.'
  - 'Ensure the final quotation or order specification matches the approved sample.'
